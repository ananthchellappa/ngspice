### 3. What the two decks do and do not prove

Stated plainly because the measurement above requires it.

**They do prove**: that the six-byte reproducer terminates normally with
`rc=1`, the three-line diagnostic on `stderr` and no signal, when the harness
is told to run it as `ngspice -b -n < deck` and as `ngspice -n deck
< /dev/null`. That is exactly criterion 1's claim for those two rows, it was
`rc=134` on both before `ce48f9630`, and the RED above is the demonstration
that these two decks catch the regression — with the guard gone they fail
three checks each, `signal` first.

**They do not prove** that the route declared in their sidecar was the route
taken. Three reasons, all measured, not argued:

- the deck is the reproducer verbatim and returns before any control language
  runs, so it cannot read `$?batchmode` or `$inputdir`, which crew 01 measured
  to be the only two discriminators that exist;
- the diagnostic and the whole of `stdout` are byte-identical on all three
  routes (measurement 1), so there is nothing to assert on;
- and the corruption test (measurement 2) confirms the consequence directly:
  drop the sidecar and the entry still passes, because for this deck the
  default `batch` route gives the same answer.

**What does hold the routes honest**, and it is elsewhere: crew 01's
`selftest-invoke.cir`, which reads `$?batchmode` from a `.control` block into
an artefact its `.files` asserts, and which fails loudly if the sidecar is read
and dropped; and the driver's `invocation:` line, printed beside every failure,
which is what made the RED above self-describing. The route *mechanism* is
asserted. The pairing of *these two decks* to *their* routes is not, and cannot
be without changing the deck into something that is no longer the reproducer.

Adding a `.control` block to make the deck witness its own route was considered

## What I falsified

### 1. The binary's build stamp does not identify the binary

The standing rule is *"State the binary's build stamp"*, and crew 01's receipt
carries a three-row table of stamps as if each named a binary. Measured: the
RED build and the restored build have **the same** Creation Date,
`Sun Aug 16 03:12:30 UTC 2026`, and **different** sha256 —
`bf671649e239e185…` against `3759a4ee11a207b4…`. Reverting `dotcards.c`,
recompiling `dotcards.lo` and relinking left the stamp untouched.

The reason is in the source: `Spice_Build_Date` is `NGSPICEBUILDDATE`
(`src/conf.c:17`), defined by `AC_DEFINE_UNQUOTED` at `configure.ac:1024` into
`build-ver_50/src/include/ngspice/config.h:481`. It moves when `config.status`
runs and at no other time. So it is a **configure**-time stamp, which is why
crew 01 saw it move three times — each was an `./autogen.sh`, not a rebuild.

This matters for exactly the measurement this batch keeps making. A crew that
reverts a guard, rebuilds, measures, restores and rebuilds — which is what a
characterisation RED *is* — will report one stamp for two different binaries
and will have recorded nothing. The stamp is still worth quoting; it is not
sufficient. This receipt quotes sha256 beside it.
